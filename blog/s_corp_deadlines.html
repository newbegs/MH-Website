Important Tax Milestones For S-Corp Owners

Published January 25, 2025

Running an S Corp means keeping a few key dates on your calendar to stay in the good graces of the IRS. The biggest milestone is March 15th, which is when your annual information return, Form 1120S, is typically due for businesses following a standard calendar year. This return generally doesn't pay taxes at the corporate level; instead, it "passes through" the income, losses, and credits to you and any other owners to report on your individual tax returns. Along with this filing, the corporation must provide each shareholder with a Schedule K-1, which summarizes their specific portion of the company's financial activity for the year. One of the most important parts of your annual routine is ensuring you are paying yourself correctly. As an S Corp owner who actively runs the business, you are required to receive a "reasonable salary," which is subject to standard payroll taxes like Social Security and Medicare. While it may be tempting to take all your earnings as profit distributions to avoid these taxes, the IRS requires that you pay yourself a wage that reflects what someone in your industry would normally earn. Any leftover profits can then be taken as distributions, which are typically free from employment taxes, providing a significant tax-saving opportunity. Beyond the federal paperwork, remember that keeping your S Corp status safe requires following regular corporate formalities, such as holding an annual meeting for shareholders. You also need to stay on top of state-specific requirements, like filing a franchise tax return or other annual reports required by your state. Neglecting these state filings can have serious consequences, such as the state suspending your corporation's rights or privileges, which can lead to expensive legal and tax headaches down the road. By staying organized with these simple steps, you can continue to enjoy the liability protection and unique tax perks that the S Corp form offers.
